GUIDE TO REGISTERING A BUSINESS IN NIGERIA
Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)

WHY REGISTER

Registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission gives a business a legal
identity. Without it a business cannot open a corporate bank account, bid for
government or corporate contracts, obtain most categories of licence, or access
formal credit from banks and development finance institutions.

THE FIRST STEP: NAME RESERVATION

The first step in setting up a business in Nigeria is to reserve a business
name with the Corporate Affairs Commission. This is done through the CAC
Company Registration Portal.

The applicant proposes two possible names, in order of preference. The
Commission checks the proposed names against existing registrations and against
its list of restricted words. If a name is available and not restricted, the
Commission issues an approval notice reserving that name for the applicant.

HOW LONG A NAME RESERVATION LASTS

A business name reservation in Nigeria lasts for 60 days from the date of
approval. Within that 60-day window the applicant must complete the full
registration. If registration is not completed before the reservation expires,
the name is released and becomes available to other applicants, and a fresh
reservation and fresh fee are required.

The 60-day reservation period applies to both business names and company names.

STEPS IN THE REGISTRATION PROCESS

Step 1  Reserve the business name on the CAC portal and obtain the approval
        notice. The reservation is valid for 60 days.

Step 2  Choose the legal structure. The main options are a Business Name for a
        sole proprietorship or partnership, a Private Company Limited by Shares
        for a company with shareholders and limited liability, and an
        Incorporated Trustees structure for non-profit organisations.

Step 3  Prepare and upload the required documents. These normally include a
        valid means of identification for each proprietor, director or trustee,
        passport photographs, the registered address of the business, and the
        proposed share capital where a company is being registered.

Step 4  Pay the prescribed filing and stamp duty fees through the portal. The
        fee depends on the structure chosen and, for companies, on the share
        capital.

Step 5  Submit the application and wait for review by the Commission. Where the
        Commission raises a query, the applicant must respond before the
        application can proceed.

Step 6  Receive the certificate of registration together with the certified
        status report, which are issued electronically.

AFTER REGISTRATION

Once the CAC certificate has been issued, the business should register with the
Federal Inland Revenue Service to obtain a Tax Identification Number, open a
corporate bank account in the registered name, and register with the relevant
state internal revenue service where it operates.

REGISTERING A CONSTRUCTION BUSINESS

A construction business follows the same CAC registration route described above.
Most construction firms register as a Private Company Limited by Shares rather
than as a Business Name, because corporate and government clients generally
require a limited liability company before awarding contracts.

In addition to CAC registration, a construction business in Nigeria typically
needs the following:

  - Registration with the Council for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria
    (COREN) where engineering services are offered, which requires that the
    firm employ registered engineers.

  - Registration with the Bureau of Public Procurement and inclusion on the
    National Database of Contractors if the firm intends to bid for federal
    government contracts.

  - Registration with the relevant state ministry of works or physical planning
    authority in each state where it intends to operate.

  - Membership of a recognised trade body such as the Federation of
    Construction Industry, which many clients treat as evidence of competence.

  - Compliance with the Employee Compensation Act through registration with the
    Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund, given the safety risks of construction
    work.

COMMON REASONS APPLICATIONS ARE REJECTED

Applications are most often rejected because the proposed name is too similar to
an existing registered name, because the name contains a restricted word such as
"Federal", "National" or "Holdings" without the required consent, because the
identification documents uploaded are unclear or expired, or because the
registered address supplied is incomplete.
